# Part 1 · The world as it stands

## 1.0 The world exists in four strata, with different authority

This is the first thing any later level must understand. The world is not one document; it is four layers written at different times under different rules, and they are not fully reconciled with one another.

| Layer               | Files                                                                                                        | Authority                                                                         | Enforced by                                                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Shipped prose**   | `docs/story/undertow/core-story.md`, `novel-complete.md`, `novel-illustrated-edition.html`, `glossary-th.md` | Highest in practice — it is written, illustrated and published                    | Nothing automated                                                  |
| **Canon**           | `content/story/canon.md`, `style.md`                                                                         | Declares itself binding on all later authoring (`canon.md` §6, `style.md` header) | Editorial review only                                              |
| **Machine content** | `content/story/*.json` (152 nodes), `content/maps/`, `content/characters/`, `content/bestiary/`              | Gate-validated for _structure_, not for _truth_                                   | `scripts/check_content.mjs`, `scripts/gen_story_graph.mjs --check` |
| **Running code**    | `colyseus-server/src/config/combat/elements.ts`, `generated/mob-types.json`, `generated/asset-keys.json`     | What actually executes                                                            | `jest`, CI                                                         |

`content/story/bible.md` is a fifth, older layer — a self-described "v0" seed (`bible.md:1`) that `content/README.md` still calls "the creative seed", and that the current canon has largely superseded without deleting. See §4 for the collisions this produces.

## 1.1 Cosmology — **none exists**

> **Resolved 2026-08-07 by I-051.** The absence recorded below was real and is now closed by
> `docs/worldbuilding/A1-cosmology.md`. **It was closed without adding a god** — the owner's
> 2026-07-23 exclusion holds at full strength, and the cosmology is a belief layer over a
> material history. Read the section below as the survey that motivated the artifact, not as
> current state.

There is no cosmology in this world. This is not an inference from silence about one specific deity; it is a total absence across every content file.

- A grep across all of `content/` and `docs/story/undertow/core-story.md` for `god`, `gods`, `divine`, `deity`, `pantheon`, `creation myth`, `heaven`, `afterlife`, `soul` returns **zero hits**.
- `core-story.md` names **no deity anywhere**, across 242 lines of world-and-plot spine.
- The exclusion was deliberate at design time: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-23-grand-epic-undertow-design.md` §5 records the Bellfaith as having "**no gods on stage, no sacrifice rites (user exclusion)**".
- The Bellfaith accordingly has **institutions but no doctrine**: bells, wardens, a seal, a Bell School, a tower district with its own gate and its own "bell-time" (`content/story/lore.json`, `lore-the-tower-that-doesnt-answer`) — and no stated belief, no scripture, no object of worship.

The one supernatural noun that touches the sacred is the element **Holy**, which exists as a damage type (`colyseus-server/src/config/combat/elements.ts:10`) and as the Bell School's branch (`canon.md §5 "Wisdom branches, schools, and towns"`), with no theology attached to it at all.

**Void** likewise has a mechanic and no metaphysics: it is what grows out of unburied battlefields (`canon.md §5 "The elements"`), what a Bellfaith bell answers (`lore.json`, `lore-the-bite-that-wont-close`), and a discipline taught from unlicensed manuals with "no roster, no master to ask when it goes wrong" (`lore.json`, `lore-a-page-from-no-school`). Nothing states what Void _is_.

## 1.2 Geography — there are two worlds, and they are not the same place

### The lore-world

Known only from prose. `regions.json` carries **no coordinates, no bounds, no adjacency and no distances** — its only quantitative field is `dangerTier` (`content/schemas/region.schema.json:17`). All spatial knowledge lives in two places:

**(a) `canon.md` §4, in words:**

- **Millcross** is "the literal hub — every road elsewhere passes through or near it" (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
- The **Northern Icefield** lies "further north past Millcross's expedition camp"; **Thornveil**'s bramble forest lies "east of Millcross" (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
- **Embervale** and **Norhollow** sit on either side of the **Ashvale plain**, "half an hour's ride apart in peacetime"; **Ashvale Front** is the contested middle, "neither town claims it, both bury their dead in it" (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
- **Gildmark** sits on the coast: 2.5 h from Millcross by the old trade road, 1.5 h from the war towns by a coastal spur. Its harbour is "the only deepwater port on this coast" (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
- **Rooktide** is inland, south of Millcross, 1 h, off the war road entirely (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
- **Cindervast** lies "beyond Ashvale Front to the north-west" (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).

The entire land is therefore crossable in a few hours — every committed road leg is ≤2 h except the Cindervast haul, per `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-15-world-rescale-design.md` §1. This is the only travel-time data in the corpus; nothing in the JSON encodes it.

Two further named grounds: **Ashvale Front** (`region-ashvale-front`, `dangerTier: high`) — the dead ground both towns bury into; and the three wilds — `region-spawn-meadow` (`safe`), `region-icefield` (`mid`), `region-thornveil` (`mid`).

Between the towns there is, explicitly, **nothing**: "ระหว่างเมืองไม่มีอะไรเลย — ไม่มีสหพันธ์ ไม่มีกฎหมายร่วม ไม่มีศาลกลาง" — no federation, no common law, no central court (`core-story.md:57`). ## 1.5 Magic, elements, schools, races and classes

### The magic model (`canon.md` §5, `style.md` §6)

1. **Magic is everyday** — widespread, ordinary, cheap. A caster spends personal mana or burns a magic stone; stones are mined in many towns and sold like any other good. "There is no shortage and no black market in it" (`canon.md §5 "The magic model"`).
2. **Runes are the real limit.** Antimagic runes are standard on war gear; ordinary combat magic fails against a warded target; only High-Tier magic breaks a ward, and High-Tier casters are few because the price is years, not money. "This is why battles are decided by steel" (`canon.md §5 "The magic model"`).
3. **Rune-craft belongs to everyone** — every town engraves its own wards; no monopoly (`canon.md §5 "The magic model"`).
4. **Nothing on the Undertow's page shows battle magic.** Every magical thing already written is utility: the bells, Gildmark's far-mirrors, lovers' ink, plus the seal's tamper-evident wax — which the text declines to call either faith or magic (`canon.md §5 "The magic model"`).

Corroborated in-world by three lore nodes: the ward is a free, competitive commodity service in Gildmark ("Warding at no charge — every shop on this street cuts the same one", `lore-a-bill-for-warded-steel`); the Elements School's fourth refusal states the ceiling plainly ("Everything on that field is warded steel, and your flame will never trouble it. Only the High-Tier will, and the High-Tier costs years, not coin", `lore-fourth-refusal`); and Void is taught only outside the system ("You will not find this written anywhere with a roof over it", `lore-a-page-from-no-school`).

### The elements — words in canon, numbers in code

Seven values, locked in three places that agree: `canon.md §5 "The elements"` (words only), `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-27-world-wisdom-design.md` (the table), and `colyseus-server/src/config/combat/elements.ts:10` (running code, with `src/tests/elements.test.ts` asserting every cell).

`ELEMENTS = ['neutral', 'earth', 'water', 'wind', 'fire', 'holy', 'void']`

- A one-directional cycle of four: water > fire > earth > wind > water. Advantage `2.0`; reverse `0.5`; same-element `0.5`.
- **Holy ⇄ Void** mutual `2.0` both directions — the only mutual pair.
- Cross-terms between the four and the pair: `1.0` in both directions.
- `neutral` is `1.0` as attacker and defender, always.

Attack element rides the weapon/coating/spell; defence element rides armour/hide/nature (`canon.md §5 "The elements"`). **War-scar monsters are Void-line**, which is why the Bell School's Holy work is a weapon and not only a mercy (`canon.md §5 "The elements"`).

`style.md` §6 rule 5 forbids any lore body, quest or dialogue line from quoting a multiplier.

### Eight classes, eight races, sixty-four legal pairings

Classes: Swordsman, Archer, Assassin, Spearman, Mage, Summoner, Engineer, Healer.
Races: Human, Demon, Dwarf, Immortal (Angel), Elf, Dragon, Beastkin, Ogre.
All 64 pairings are canon; none forbidden, none correct. Each race leans in one direction only — Human balanced, Ogre power/HP, Demon Void, Dragon elemental power, Elf mana/cast speed, Dwarf defence/craft, Immortal Holy, Beastkin agility (`canon.md §5 "The eight classes and the eight races"`).

**Explicitly not in game state:** "The server stores no class, race, or job on a player… No canon text, quest, dialogue, or lore body may assume a player's class or race exists in game state" (`canon.md §5 "Not in game state yet"`).

Time is kept only in seasons, "a generation", "ten years of this log" (`lore-a-decade-of-meals`), an internal camp count "Year one, count one: forty tents, sixty mouths, one working well" (`lore-the-first-tally`), and Rooktide's before/after.

### How news travels (`canon.md` §4)

Three layers, two speeds — the world's single most developed system.

1. **Bells signal.** Simple codes only: danger, war, all-clear, "assemble for a proclamation". A bell never carries detail.
2. **Bell-wardens proclaim.** The detail is read aloud beneath the tower; people trust it because the sacred toll summoned them.
3. **The bell-seal certifies provenance, not truth.** Inter-town proclamations carry weight only when stamped, but what the stamp attests is that a statement was given to the Bellfaith and recorded — never that it is accurate (F-035). "An unsealed proclamation is just a rumor with good staging"; a sealed one is a rumor somebody put their name to. The seal is state news agency and notary in one, and the notary half is literal. Forging it is possible and worthless.

**Fast signals (hours)** — each town's own bell relays layer-1 codes to the next town's along the trade roads, reaching every town on the network within hours. **Slow detail** — the sealed proclamation travels the same roads by bell-rider or message-bird, at the same pace as any other road traffic (every committed road leg is now ≤2 h except the Cindervast haul).

**The exploit:** the gap between a bare category and the full sealed detail is where truth is decided; whoever's version of the detail arrives first is the version that sticks. The Broker's riders always ride it faster, and riders carrying inconvenient proclamations get robbed "unusually often" (`canon.md §4 "How news travels (the Bellfaith, three layers, two speeds)"`).

**Gildmark's edge:** the **far-mirrors**, a Gildmark monopoly, are the one channel faster than the fast signal — a private line outside the relay chain entirely (`canon.md §4 "How news travels (the Bellfaith, three layers, two speeds)"`). One harbour ledger line values them above the entire fleet (`lore-the-second-harbor-entry`).

## 1.10 The machinery that enforces the world

Worth recording, because it determines what is cheap and what is expensive to change.

- **`scripts/check_content.mjs`** — loads all nine story files into one flat global id map; hard-FAILs on duplicate ids, schema violations (`additionalProperties: false` everywhere), dangling or wrong-kind typed refs, quests without objectives/giver/arc, arcs without quests, duplicate `event.timelineOrder`, act `order` not unique-and-contiguous 1..N, `diedAt` set while `status` is alive, `unlockedBy` cycles (DFS over the quest/event/dialogue graph), and any `mob:*` reference not in `generated/mob-types.json`.
- **WARNs** (escalated by `--require-complete`): orphan characters, orphan factions, unreachable quests. **Never-escalated WARNs:** an event whose triggering quest sits in a later act, and any lore thread with fewer than 2 fragments.
- **`scripts/gen_story_graph.mjs --check`** — a drift gate: the committed Mermaid at `docs/story/story-graph.md` must match a fresh regeneration.
- **The `links[]` field on every node is documented as loose and unvalidated** — it is not an edge.
- **Untyped by design:** the bestiary (not read at all), `content/story/bible.md` (prose), `canon.md` and `style.md` (editorial only — `style.md:6-8` states there is "no schema or gate machinery behind it").

# Part 5 · The scale question — evidence only

The decision before the owner: whether the Undertow remains **the world's story**, or is re-scoped to **one region's story inside a much larger MMO world**.

The charter (§1) already records "World scale: **Large**, comparable to a major MMO continent; may start small and scale" as a standing decision, and "Revision scope: **Everything on the table**". This section does not revisit those. It lays out what the existing content says, in four columns of evidence. **No recommendation is made or implied.**

## 5.1 What in the existing content assumes a small frontier

1. **The population table totals roughly 42,000 living people** across all six towns, with the largest city at 20,000 and the smallest at 1,500 (`core-story.md:61-68`).
2. **"This is a war of towns, not of a kingdom, so every corpse has a name, and every name has a family."** Forces are "หลักร้อยถึงพัน ไม่ใช่หมื่น" — hundreds to a thousand, not tens of thousands (`core-story.md:59`).
3. **No standing armies existed before the war** — only town guards numbering in the dozens, because after Cindervast the land feared armies as much as central power (`core-story.md:59`).
4. **Nothing exists between the towns** — no federation, no common law, no central court; only two cross-border institutions, both destroyed in act 1 (`core-story.md:57`).
5. **The whole land is crossable in a few hours.** Embervale to Norhollow is half an hour's ride; Millcross to Gildmark 2.5 h; the war towns to Gildmark 1.5 h; Millcross to Rooktide 1 h (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
6. **A single bell relay chain reaches every town on the network within hours** of the first bell ringing (`canon.md §4 "How news travels (the Bellfaith, three layers, two speeds)"`). The Broker's entire method is the gap between a bare category and the full sealed detail this produces.
7. **Millcross is "the literal hub — every road elsewhere passes through or near it"** (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`).
8. **Gildmark is the only deepwater port on the coast and the land's only door to the outside world** (`canon.md §4 "Geography & trade logic"`; `core-story.md:26`).
9. **One man's horses cover the whole land.** The Broker's riders "always ride that gap faster than anyone else's" on every road (`canon.md §4 "How news travels (the Bellfaith, three layers, two speeds)"`).
10. **One woman knows every family's dead.** The Widow "ตระเวนค่ายผู้ลี้ภัยทุกเมือง จำชื่อคนตายของทุกครอบครัว" — walks every refugee camp in every town and remembers every family's dead, and can raise a mob "ในเมืองไหนก็ได้" within an hour (`core-story.md:106`).
11. **Rooktide fell to ~300 people and returned to ~2,500.** The town's entire identity is that arithmetic (`core-story.md:67`).
12. **Six towns, six governments, each fully described** — the political map has no unknowns and no room for more (`core-story.md:46-55`).
13. **The playable map is a single 1000×1000-unit shelf** with three regions and no towns at all (`content/maps/atlas-frontier.md`).
14. **The Thai glossary defines each town by its role in this one war** — "เมืองสงครามฝั่ง A / ฝั่ง B", "เมืองท่าค้าอาวุธชายฝั่ง" (`glossary-th.md`).

## 5.2 What would survive a continent unchanged

1. **The story never claims to be the world.** Its consistent scope noun is "แผ่นดินนี้" — _this land_ — never "the world". The land is a bounded thing inside something larger by its own grammar.
2. **A door to the outside is already open and unused.** Once a year, ships from "แผ่นดินโพ้นทะเล" (lands across the sea) put in at Gildmark on the trade wind and carry goods on to "ดินแดนไกลที่ไม่มีใครในเรื่องนี้เคยเห็น" — far lands **nobody in this story has ever seen** (`core-story.md:26`). Nothing beyond that sentence has been spent. **PARTIALLY RESOLVED 2026-08-15 (F-043).** The door is now charted to a named far shore — Tallowquay on Coldreach, by mariners' report (`A2-wider-world.md`); still used once a year, still unused as an expansion.
3. **The word "continent" is already on the page.** `bible.md:9`: "Atlas is a fractured frontier **continent**."
4. **No cosmology binds the world's size at the top.** There is no god, no creation, no world-shape — nothing that fixes how much world there is (§1.1).
5. **The magic model was written to outlive the epic.** `canon.md §5 "Magic, schools, and the elements"`: "This section is world law **for all content authored after it, not only for the Undertow acts**."
6. **The element system is placeless.** Seven elements and a multiplier table in running code, tied to no geography (`elements.ts`).
7. **Eight races and eight classes describe a population far more varied than six towns of ~42,000** — and 64 committed images already assume that roster (`canon.md` §5; `art-manifest.json`).
8. **The bestiary is already sized past the frontier.** 116 monsters, 13 body plans, 12 families, 9 regions, and a 1–80 level pyramid — an MMO progression spine that no currently playable region can host.
9. **The precedent for nesting already exists.** `canon.md §1 "World chronology"` folds the entire pre-existing meadow/icefield seed content in as "Millcross-side ground… its immediate frontier" — a small area absorbed into a larger frame without rewriting it.
10. **The two worlds have never been joined, so nothing has been spent either way.** The reconciliation is recorded as an open, deferred decision (`world-art-bible-program.md` §3, §6.1).
11. **Several actors are unpinned.** The Cindered diaspora, the Ashen Column, the Ash Prophet, the Stoneguard and the Broker's rider network are all mobile or stateless; none is defined by a coordinate.
12. **The theme is not scale-dependent.** "Truth can beat a lie but cannot erase the hatred the lie planted" is a claim about people, not about territory (`core-story.md:222`).

## 5.3 What would strain

1. **The bell relay.** One chain reaching six towns in hours is plausible over a few days' travel. Across a continent it needs a tower count, a maintenance cost and an owner — and the hours-versus-days gap that is the Broker's whole method changes shape with distance.
2. **Gildmark's monopoly weakens the moment a second coast exists.** "Whoever controls the door to the sea controls the price of the whole land" is a statement about a land with exactly one door.
3. **"Nothing between the towns."** At frontier scale this is a striking fact. At continent scale it becomes an absence that demands an explanation — why has no one built a federation across a continent in a hundred years?
4. **Millcross as the hub through which every road passes** cannot hold across a continent without a second hub, which weakens it.
5. **Armies of hundreds** are believable for towns of thousands. Over a continent they read as skirmishes — and canon has already ruled out standing armies as a matter of post-Last-King trauma.
6. **The 1–80 level pyramid over 9 regions** gives roughly one level band per region as it stands. Stretched over a continent, either the bands thin out or the regions multiply past what the bestiary covers.
7. **Personal reach.** Both the Widow's network of the bereaved and the Broker's rider network are described as covering _everywhere_. Over a continent, "everywhere" needs infrastructure neither character has.
8. **The Bellfaith as the only church.** One institution with no rival and no schism is credible across a week's travel; across a continent, its uniqueness becomes the remarkable fact rather than the background.
9. **The Cindered diaspora of ~3,000 "scattered through every town"** is a visible minority across six towns. Across a continent it disappears.

## 5.4 What would break

1. **The population table.** ~42,000 living people is not a continent's population. The figures interlock: 92% of 40,000 erased carries Cindervast's entire moral weight; 300 → 2,500 _is_ Rooktide's identity; 1,500 tripling to 4,000+ _is_ the Quartermaster's burden; ~12% levy is the stated realistic ceiling. Changing any one invalidates the rest.
2. **"A war of towns, not of a kingdom, so every corpse has a name."** This sentence is the stated licence for the story's entire intimate register. Continent scale makes it false, and the theme — that the only thing crossing the front line is a bond between two named people — rests on it.
3. **~~Exactly one king in all recorded history, and "king" now a curse.~~ REMOVED 2026-08-07 (I-051, `DR-006`).** The exclusivity claim and the taboo are gone from every file, including the shipped novel and its illustrated edition. What remains, carrying the same weight: the Last King **of Cindervast**, the relic weapon, the Stoneguard, the Cindered, the Ash Prophet and act 4's stake. The land refuses **central power**, not a word.
4. **Gildmark as the single door to the sea.** It cannot survive a continent with more than one coastline. It is the causal root of the Broker's plot, the arms trade, the far-mirror monopoly, the war economy, and the reason the Brotherhood Caravan existed at all.
5. **The Bellfaith reaching every town, and "whoever owns the bells owns the truth."** A single controllable news monopoly over six towns is the mechanism of the entire information plot. A continent-spanning monopoly is a different institution with different politics.
6. **The shipped six-town map.** A hand-drawn SVG in a published document showing six towns, one sea and one plain, with no space drawn for anything else.
7. **The Thai glossary's town descriptors**, which define each of the six towns by its role in this one war — an append-only table whose rule is "never silently change a rendering."
8. **The Brotherhood Caravan as the land's single peace institution.** One convoy holding the peace of an entire continent is not the same claim as one convoy holding the peace between two neighbouring towns.

## 5.5 The two facts that most constrain the choice

Stated plainly, without recommendation:

- **The story's scale is written into its numbers, not just its mood.** ~42,000 people, armies of hundreds, a land crossable in under a week, and one port — and each of those is load-bearing for a specific plot mechanism, not decoration.
- **The expansion door is already open and costs nothing to use.** One sentence establishes lands across the sea that no one in the story has seen; the word "continent" is already in `bible.md`; the magic system was written to outlive the epic; and the bestiary and the 64-image class grid already describe more world than the six towns can hold.

The decision is the owner's.
