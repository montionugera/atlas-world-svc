## 4. Uncertainty notes — what is contested or unverified

<div class="callout danger">
<strong>Read this before building on any ⚠️ or ❓ row.</strong>
</div>

**(a) Ghent / _Roeland_, 1540.** ❓ The _Caroline Concession_, the confiscations and the fine are
solidly attested. The bell's fate is muddled: sources disagree on whether it was destroyed or
merely taken down, and the popular story that Charles V personally cut it up is explicitly
described as untrue. I have **not** consulted a scholarly monograph on the revolt. Treat "the
bell was removed as part of a formal punishment package" as safe; treat everything about its
physical fate as unsettled.

**(b) Tocsin as capital offence.** ⚠️ The "capital offence to sound without government orders"
phrasing traces through Carlyle-era English sources rather than a cited statute. The Castilian
case (Hernández 2024) is the **verified** version of this mechanism; use that one.

**(c) The 1418 Bath precedence dispute.** ⚠️ Reported in the Wikipedia _Curfew bell_ article
and in Andrews' _Old Church Lore_ (a 19th-century antiquarian compilation — useful but not
peer-reviewed). The general phenomenon of precedence disputes is well attested (Corbin);
the specific 1418 case is not independently verified here.

**(d) Bell-ringer lightning deaths.** ⚠️ Two incompatible figures circulate: **121 killed
1750–1783** (attributed directly to Fischer 1784) and **103 killed in France 1753–1786**.
Both come to me via secondary web sources; I have not seen Fischer's text. The _bans_
(Prussia/Austria/Bavaria 1783; Parlement de Paris 1787) are consistently reported but likewise
unverified against statute. Use the mechanism; do not quote the numbers as fact.

**(e) The Uglich bell.** ⚠️ Extremely consistently reported (weight ~300 kg, 120 lashes,
clapper and ears removed, ~1,400 miles, c. 60 families, one year, Tobolsk inscription) but my
sources are popular-history sites, not a Russian-archival study. The **event** is not in doubt;
the specific numbers should be treated as traditional rather than measured.

**(f) _Klokslag_ / jurisdiction-by-audibility.** ⚠️ The Dutch-language territorial sense of
_klokslag_ is asserted by a non-academic source; the Berlin Topoi research project C-6-7
independently establishes that bell sound structured juridical and social space in the Middle
Ages. The concept is sound; the specific etymological claim is unverified.

**(g) WWII bell confiscation figures.** ⚠️ ~175,000 requisitioned / ~150,000 never returned
come from museum-adjacent and press sources (National Bell Festival, Britannica _Today in
History_), not a scholarly census. Order of magnitude is safe; exact figures are not.

**(h) The Blanc brothers, 1834–36.** ⚠️ The case is real and widely retold (Schneier, Standage's
_The Victorian Internet_), and the mechanism — bribed operator, error-plus-backspace
steganography, telescope watcher, no applicable law — is consistent across sources. I have
**not** seen a French primary or scholarly account. The claim "five days ahead of the mail"
and the outcome "fine for bribery only" both come from the same secondary chain.

**(i) Reuter's pigeons, 1850.** ⚠️ Figures vary between sources (45 pigeons; 76 miles / ~120 km;
"about two hours"; "six hours faster than rail"). Britannica confirms the service and the
Aachen–Brussels gap; the operational numbers are popular-history.

**(j) Rothschild and Waterloo.** ❓ **Actively contested.** The dramatic version is refuted
(Cathcart 2015). What survives is modest and still debated: a courier arrived ahead of the
official despatch; whether Nathan traded on it is disputed. Do not use this as an example of a
successful information monopoly — use it as an example of how such a story is _manufactured_.

**(k) Cunning-folk numbers.** ⚠️ "Several thousand in 19th-century England" is attributed to
Owen Davies via a search summary; I did not read the book. Thomas's argument about the
Reformation opening a market gap is well established and safe.

**Could not verify at all:**

- **A medieval (pre-1500) bell-casting contract price.** Every hard number in §1 is
  **1527–1591 London**. The claim "casting a bell was a major, once-a-generation parish capital
  expense" is well supported for the 16th century; extrapolating to the 13th is an assumption,
  not a finding.
- **Any legal monopoly on public crying**, i.e. a statute fining unauthorised proclamation.
  Town criers were appointed by mayor or lord of the manor and their messages were legally
  binding, but I found **no** source for a penalty on someone else crying news. The frequently
  repeated claim that harming a town crier was **treason** appears only in popular-history
  sites and should be treated as folklore until sourced.
- **A per-village density figure for cunning folk.** Only a national estimate was found.

## 5. Sharpest hooks

The five mechanisms with the most fantasy potential, and — per the contract's swap test —
what each gives you that the usual fantasy-church treatment does not.

1. **The death-knell price list (St Andrew Hubbard, 1527/1546).** _Not a cliché because:_
   the sacred rite is a **priced product line** with a free tier, and the institution's
   incentive is to sell **more** ritual, not less — funerals were 14% of parish income against
   6% of cost. The usual fantasy church is funded by mystery; this one is funded by a tariff
   card, and its doctrine bends around its revenue.

2. **The illicit _rebato_ and its punishment (Castile, 1720–1789).** _Not a cliché because:_
   the contested resource is not the bell but the **right to convene a crowd**. A commoner ringing
   for six hours is prosecuted as _equivalent to the riot itself_ — the sentence (head in an iron
   cage atop the tower he rang) makes the tower the gallows. This gives you religious authority
   as a **crowd-summoning monopoly** rather than a moral one.

3. **The Blanc brothers' erased character (1834).** _Not a cliché because:_ the corruption is
   invisible and rides **inside authentic official traffic** — the state carries the private
   message at its own expense, and the payload is the part that gets deleted. That is a far more
   interesting corrupt-institution mechanic than a bought official, and it leaves no evidence
   in the archive by construction.

4. **Saint Guinefort's wood — a banned cult that outlives its inquisition by six centuries.**
   _Not a cliché because:_ the folk practice is not persecuted-and-heroic and not
   defeated-and-tragic. It is **beneath notice**, attached to a place rather than a building, run
   by women, about sick children, and simply **outlasts** everyone sent to end it. It gives you
   heterodoxy as boring civic infrastructure instead of as forbidden power.

5. **Storm-ringing: the protective ritual that kills its own performers (Fischer, 1784).**
   _Not a cliché because:_ the ritual is not fake and not real — it is **actively lethal to the
   people who perform it**, defended for centuries on protective grounds, and stopped by the
   _secular_ power over the institution's practice. That inverts the usual "church suppresses,
   state permits" arrangement, and gives the ringer's office a real, measurable mortality rate.

**Runner-up worth flagging:** _jurisdiction defined by audibility_ (§1, ⚠️) — the border of the
law is wherever the sound still carries, so it moves with the weather. Verify the _klokslag_
claim before building on it.
